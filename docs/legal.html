Who runs this site

This website presents Kairo, a free and open-source program. It’s run by the maintainer of the Kairo project on GitHub, github.com/fsiuhgshiqoih32. It isn’t a business: nothing is sold here, and there are no ads or sponsors.

To get in touch, for example about a problem, a licence or something on this site, open an issue on GitHub.

Privacy on this website

This site sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. It has no analytics, no tracking pixels, no ads, no embedded videos or social buttons, and loads no fonts or scripts from other companies: everything it shows is served from the site itself.

Hosting

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages by GitHub, Inc. Like any web server, GitHub receives your IP address and browser details when you open a page. GitHub says it logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes. See About GitHub Pages and the GitHub General Privacy Statement. Requests about those logs go to GitHub.

The download picker

To offer the newest version, the page asks GitHub’s public API (api.github.com) which release is the latest. Your browser makes that request directly, so GitHub sees it the same way it sees the page itself. Nothing about you is sent with it. The downloads themselves come from GitHub Releases.

The “send the link to myself” button

On phones, this button opens your device’s own share menu, or copies the page’s address. The site doesn’t see what you do with it.

Privacy in the Kairo app

Kairo records your screen and sound on your computer and keeps the recordings there. You don’t need an account, and there are no analytics, no telemetry and no crash reporting. Kairo goes online only for these things:

  • Update checks. When its window opens, Kairo asks GitHub’s API for the newest release, identifying itself as Kairo and its version. Updates are downloaded from GitHub and checked against their published checksums. You can turn update checks off in Settings.
  • Speech recognition. The first time you turn on voice clipping or captions, Kairo downloads the Vosk library from GitHub and its English model from alphacephei.com. After that, speech is recognised on your computer and never sent anywhere.
  • Share links, when you ask for one. Kairo uploads that clip to catbox.moe, or to litterbox.catbox.moe for a link that expires, and copies the link. Anyone with the link can watch the clip. These are independent services with their own terms.
  • Friends, if you sign in. Signing in with Google gives Kairo’s friends server your Google account’s ID, email address and name. The server runs on Supabase, in the United States. It keeps the username you pick, your friend requests and friendships, and the messages and clip links you send and receive, so that your friends’ Kairo can show them. Only you and that friend can read a message through Kairo, but messages aren’t end-to-end encrypted, so whoever runs the server could read them. Clips you send are uploaded to catbox.moe, as with share links. Kairo checks for new messages about every 30 seconds while it runs. Signing out removes the account from your computer. To delete your account and everything the server keeps about you, open an issue on GitHub with your username.
  • Discord, if you connect a channel. Kairo posts the clips you choose to that channel through the webhook you gave it. The webhook is stored on your computer.

The keys and clicks overlay, when you turn it on, remembers only the gaming keys you press (WASD, QERF, Space, Shift, Ctrl, Tab and 1 to 4) and your mouse buttons while recording. Nothing else you type is kept.

You decide what you record and share. Other people’s voices in voice chat may need their permission before you record or post them, depending on where you live. Please also follow the rules of the games and services you use.
